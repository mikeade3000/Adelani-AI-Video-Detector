WHAT JUST HAPPENED

Debrief

You scanned a QR code and, after one consent tap, a web page collected device details and showed a convincing “locked” screen. A real attacker would skip the consent, hide the exit, and dress the page up as your bank, employer, or a fine to pay.

Defensive takeaways
  • Preview a QR code’s URL before opening it; treat unexpected QR codes like unexpected links.
  • A web page can never truly lock your phone — the power button and app switcher always work.
  • Browsers reveal more than people expect (user-agent, timezone, screen size). That’s normal fingerprinting, not a breach.
  • “Pay now to unlock” is scareware. Don’t pay; close the tab.
